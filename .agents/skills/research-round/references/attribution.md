# Interpreting attribution

Read with single-run reports and registered comparisons before explaining a result or choosing
the next hypothesis. Reports supply the slices, registered prediction signs and any declared
interaction; attribution does not change the account test.

## Evidence scope

Cite the slice and measure behind each conclusion. For every registered prediction, state whether
its observed sign held or why it cannot be checked. A favorable account result does not rescue a
failed mechanism prediction; matching signs are directional consistency, not causal or statistical
proof. Several duties sharing a measure share one observation.

The source change is relative to the parent; the comparison is against the registered control.
Call it an ablation only if the actual source difference isolates the declared change. A coupled
rule affecting several duties estimates a policy contrast, not each duty's independent effect.

Trade pairing describes the populations that changed. Read coverage before matched contributions;
use only the product's pairs and account daily returns for inference. Derive additional tables
through `nautilus-report-analysis`, not extra attribution replays.

## Economic interpretation

Relate profit sources and implementation costs to exposure, holding behavior and the conditions
under which outcomes occurred. Use each measure's population, units and definition notes before
assigning meaning. Changes in exposure, marked prices, compounding, costs or opportunity mix can
change a statistic without identifying a better economic rule. Concentration and state slices
locate questions; they do not determine a filter, parameter direction or causal explanation.

Connect the observed pattern to the strategy policy that could produce it, using the duty header
and directory to locate responsibility. State competing explanations and what a proposed change
could distinguish. An unexplored duty is a design choice to consider, not evidence that adding it
will help. Technical engine guarantees do not certify an economic policy.

## Interactions

Declare the four corners of a two-change comparison in the registration that adds the last
corner. Use the product's interaction to explain that fixed contrast, never as improvement
acceptance; do not select corners retrospectively. Contributions along a lineage depend on change
order, and a four-corner result is conditional on its settings and window, not independent data
or a universal effect.
