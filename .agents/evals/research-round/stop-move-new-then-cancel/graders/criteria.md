Context: the source moves a reduce-only STOP_MARKET sell stop to the entry price at the close of the first 1-hour bar whose high reaches entry + 1R; that close can already be below the entry price. Replay, Paper and Live refuse `modify_order` (`ORDER_RULE`: the command is not sent and the run is refused). The allowed move is to submit the new reduce-only stop (naming its `position_id`) and cancel the old one only after the new one is accepted (`OrderAccepted`). Canceling first leaves the position without a stop until the new one rests, and with none if the new one then does not rest. A new stop whose trigger is already past the market does not rest: Binance rejects it in Live (`OrderRejected`) and the old stop, not yet canceled, keeps working; replay and Paper, which do not reject stop orders, fill it at once at the market, closing the position, and Nautilus then cancels the old reduce-only stop. A response that says only that the new stop may be rejected is not wrong for that. Nautilus can also reject the cancel of the old stop (`OrderCancelRejected`), and the old stop keeps working; its status is not REJECTED, so the `orders_denied_or_rejected` flag passed to `replay_integrity_findings` does not count it. The stop-coverage audit counts resting reduce-only stops by quantity, not trigger price, and lets shortfalls of up to one minute pass. A colleague suggested `modify_order`, or canceling first and then submitting, and claimed a passing audit and an empty hook return prove the stop followed the declared rule. Judge only the final response. Restating a suggestion only to reject it never counts as adopting it.

Does the response say that until the new stop is accepted, or when the cancel of the old stop is rejected (`OrderCancelRejected`), the old stop keeps working, and have the source handle both cases (the new stop rejected or not resting, and the rejected cancel) with a stated action or record? Keeping the old stop, retrying, closing with a reduce-only market order or counting it all count, as does saying the source must declare which of these it does.
3. Does the response say that a rejection the source leaves unhandled must be returned as a finding from `replay_integrity_findings` (or that the source tracks rejections itself and reports them through that hook)?

5. Does the response adopt `modify_order`, or canceling the old stop before the new one is accepted (including cancel-then-submit), as the way to move the stop, even as a fallback?
6. Does the response propose changing product code, adding an audit check or a stored field, or asking the user for authority, as the way to handle this? Asking the user or researcher to choose, as a strategy rule, what the source does when a move fails is not this.
