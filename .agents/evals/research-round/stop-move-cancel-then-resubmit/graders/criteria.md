Context: the source moves a reduce-only STOP_MARKET sell stop to the entry price at the close of the first 1-hour bar whose high reaches entry + 1R; that close can already be below the entry price. Replay, Paper and Live refuse `modify_order` (`ORDER_RULE`: the command is not sent and the run is refused). One position's working reduce-only stops may hold at most its quantity (`ORDER_RULE`), so a new full-size stop submitted while the old one still works is refused and the run with it. The allowed move is to cancel the old stop and submit the new reduce-only stop for the position (naming its `position_id`) on the old one's `OrderCanceled`; the position is briefly without a stop, which the audit tolerates for up to one minute. Nautilus can reject the cancel (`OrderCancelRejected`): the old stop keeps working, and its status is not REJECTED, so the `orders_denied_or_rejected` flag passed to `replay_integrity_findings` does not count it. A new stop whose trigger is already past the market does not rest: Binance rejects it in Live (`OrderRejected`) and the position then has no stop; replay and Paper, which do not reject stop orders, fill it at once at the market. Either way the source closes the position with a reduce-only order (or submits the stop only when its trigger is not yet passed, and otherwise closes). The stop-coverage audit counts resting reduce-only stops by quantity, not trigger price, and lets shortfalls of up to one minute pass. A colleague suggested `modify_order`, or submitting the new stop first and canceling the old once the new one is accepted, and claimed a passing audit and an empty hook return prove the stop followed the declared rule. Judge only the final response. Restating a suggestion only to reject it never counts as adopting it.

1. Does the response move the stop by canceling the old stop first and submitting the new reduce-only stop at the entry price, for the position's quantity, on the old stop's cancel event (`OrderCanceled`)?
2. Does the response say that a rejected cancel (`OrderCancelRejected`) leaves the old stop working, and that when the new stop is rejected or its trigger is already passed the position is without a stop, and have the source handle both with a stated action or record? Keeping the old stop or retrying the cancel, and closing with a reduce-only order or checking the trigger before submitting, all count, as does saying the source must declare which it does.
3. Does the response say that a rejection the source leaves unhandled must be returned as a finding from `replay_integrity_findings` (or that the source tracks rejections itself and reports them through that hook)?
4. Does the response claim that the passing audit, the `orders_denied_or_rejected` flag or an empty hook return shows every break-even move happened as declared, or that the source need not watch the results of these orders?
5. Does the response adopt `modify_order`, or submitting the new stop while the old one still works (including new-then-cancel after `OrderAccepted`), as the way to move the stop, even as a fallback?
6. Does the response propose changing product code, adding an audit check or a stored field, or asking the user for authority, as the way to handle this? Asking the user or researcher to choose, as a strategy rule, what the source does when a move fails is not this.
